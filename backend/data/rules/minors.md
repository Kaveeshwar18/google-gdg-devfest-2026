## Annexure C and Annexure D: which consent form

Passport Seva uses two parental declarations for minors:

- **Annexure D** is for normal cases, where both parents sign. At the passport office, either both parents attend, or one parent attends with both parents' passports.
- **Annexure C** is for a married parent who cannot get the other parent's consent, whatever the reason. Reasons include:
  - the other parent refusing on purpose
  - desertion
  - no contact between separated parents
  - ex-parte divorce proceedings
  - the other parent living or travelling abroad

Annexure D also covers three other situations:
- a single parent with full custody and no visitation rights for the other parent
- a seafaring spouse who cannot sign
- unmarried parents who file the Appendix 12 affidavit

If both parents are in divorce proceedings, the court's permission or both parents' consent on Annexure D is needed instead of Annexure C. The Annexure C form asks for supporting papers for the reason chosen. For example, a parent abroad needs a passport copy and proof of residence or travel abroad. The passport office decides whether the papers are enough.

## Single, separated and divorced parents

The application form lets the applicant give only one parent's name, the father, mother or legal guardian, so single parents can apply. The parent with custody supplies the details whatever the marriage status: divorced, divorce pending, separated or deserted, with or without visitation rights for the other parent.

- **Divorce still pending:** the parent gets the court's permission or files Annexure C with the reason.
- **Exclusive custody without visitation rights:** a certified copy of the court order goes in, with Annexure C signed by the custodial parent.
- **Separated without a formal divorce:** an Annexure C declaration from the parent with custody may be accepted.

## Unwed parents and children born outside marriage

For a child of an unwed mother, whether or not the father is known, the mother files Annexures C and D and the father's name is left blank. When a child is born outside marriage, including to parents married to other people, both biological parents can be named on the passport if they both sign Annexure D. In it they confirm the relationship and that the child was born of it. Where a married parent has been abandoned by the other parent after conception or birth, the parent with custody files Annexure C.

## Step-parent names and children without a birth record

A minor whose custodial parent has remarried can have the step-parent's name printed instead of the biological parent's. This needs:
- a plain-paper self-declaration that accepts responsibility for any future litigation
- at least two educational or public documents showing the step-parent as parent
- the registered certificate of the remarriage
- the biological parent's death certificate, if they have died

A child who has lived in an orphanage or child care home and has no proof of date of birth can submit a declaration of the date of birth from the head of the home, on its letterhead.

## Tatkaal and police verification for minors

Tatkaal is not available for a minor's application filed with Annexure C. It is also not available for minors with a single parent, children born outside marriage with a single parent, or adopted and surrogate children. The police verification FAQ explains when minors are generally exempt from verification. That FAQ names the legal guardian's declaration Annexure C, while the annexure list on the portal names the declaration affirming a minor's particulars Annexure I. Check which form the passport office wants before the appointment.
